Dear Dr. Babu and Reviewers,

We thank the Editor and the reviewers for their careful and constructive assessment. We agree that the original manuscript overinterpreted CTCF motif predictions as evidence of promoter-enhancer regulation, did not adequately address sequencing-depth dependence, and described the pooled calls too strongly as high-confidence or consensus regulatory interactions.

In response, we performed a substantial reanalysis. The revised study will be framed as a **pooled rat frontal-cortex chromatin-loop annotation resource with layered structural and putative regulatory evidence**, rather than as a set of validated promoter-enhancer interactions. The main changes are: (i) complete depth normalization of all ten Hi-C libraries to 140 million valid contacts followed by reconstruction of `.hic` files and rerunning HiCCUPS at 5, 10, and 25 kb; (ii) removal of the `CTCF >=6` criterion from loop selection, regulatory categories, directional assignment, and downstream gene analyses; (iii) strand-aware TSS reconstruction and interval-based promoter/TSS annotation; (iv) integration of an external rat prefrontal-cortex snATAC-seq peak catalog as orthogonal open-chromatin evidence; (v) a 1,000-permutation matched-anchor null analysis for the ATAC annotation; and (vi) removal of the previous Top-54-gene emphasis and restriction of GO analysis to exploratory interpretation.

We respond point by point below.

## Referee 1

### General Comment

> The authors present a catalog of putative enhancer-promoter loops in the rat frontal cortex across multiple strains. They claim to identify over 30,000 loops, which they then refine to over 15,000 loops mediating enhancer-promoter interactions, and they identify an association between loops and developmentally regulated genes. This resource would enable researchers who use rats as a model organism (regardless of strain) to examine if genes of interest have enhancer-promoter loops, enabling future studies in this system. However, only existing Hi-C data is analyzed, and the manuscript makes assumptions regarding genomic features of the rat genome without experimental data or references to support them. They also focus on CTCF loops while ignoring other loops which are more likely to regulate gene expression. Ultimately, it is difficult to make meaningful conclusions from the data presented because only Hi-C data is included and there is no manipulation aside from rat strain, and this sole point of variation receives only superficial consideration. As a result, there is little biological insight generated by the data presented. Additional experiments would be required to draw meaningful conclusions from this data.

**Response:** We agree that the original framing exceeded what could be supported by the available data. We therefore no longer present the resource as approximately 15,000 high-confidence or functionally validated promoter-enhancer interactions. The 31,021 primary records are now defined as exact, resolution-specific, pooled HiCCUPS call records shorter than 2 Mb, with source-library support and HiCCUPS QC fields retained. They are not described as 31,021 independent physical loops, consensus loops, or conserved loops.

The regulatory annotation is now evidence layered. Direct promoter/TSS overlap and TSS-excluded ATAC overlap are reported separately, dual-promoter contacts are retained rather than forced into a promoter-enhancer direction, and predicted CTCF motifs are used only as structural sequence annotation. The mutually exclusive resource categories are:

| Category | Calls | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Direct promoter/TSS at one anchor with >=50 bp opposite-anchor TSS-excluded ATAC | 10,469 | Open-chromatin-supported putative promoter-to-distal-element contact |
| Direct promoter/TSS at both anchors | 4,048 | Promoter-promoter-compatible contact with secondary ATAC-pattern annotation |
| Direct promoter/TSS at one anchor without >=50 bp opposite-anchor TSS-excluded ATAC | 1,826 | Single-promoter contact without the specified distal open-chromatin support |
| No direct promoter/TSS at either anchor | 14,678 | Unassigned or lower-regulatory-evidence resource call |

These categories total 31,021 records. ATAC-seq is interpreted as open-chromatin support, not as proof of enhancer activity, target-gene regulation, or promoter-enhancer function. The absence of sample-matched expression, active-enhancer histone marks, CTCF ChIP-seq, and perturbation experiments will be stated as a central limitation. GO enrichment will be presented only as exploratory functional context.

### Major Comment 1

> In the first part of the manuscript, the authors examine data from 10 rat strains. Overall, the authors find a “high degree of sharing, suggesting a conserved chromatin topology among these related strains”. They also state that strains with fewer connections “reflect greater biological divergence, combined with artifacts of technical variability” (Page 14, line 4). However, the genetic relationship between the rat strains is not considered, and there is no evidence that differences in loop overlap are related to genetic differences between strains. Indeed, it is clear that technical variability is a major concern. The authors clearly show that sequencing depth is the main driver of variability of loop number (which is not surprising). To account for this technical variation (sequencing depth), the authors could downsample their Hi-C matrices to the same number of contacts prior to analysis. However, even after this is complete it is not clear how metrics presented, such as pairwise overlap in number of loops between individual strains, meaningfully informs the comparison of DNA looping among 10 different strains. A more meaningful approach could start by building a consensus loop set, or by examining the link between looping and genetic variation across strains (e.g. by examining SNPs in CTCF sites).

**Response:** We agree that the original strain-level interpretation was not supported and that sequencing depth was a major confounder. We therefore completed a full depth-normalization reanalysis of all ten libraries. Starting from Juicer `merged_nodups.txt` files, we retained contacts for which both reads had MAPQ >=30 and excluded intra-fragment pairs. Because these inputs were duplicate removed, PCR duplicates were not sampled as independent contacts. Each library was uniformly downsampled to exactly 140,000,000 valid contacts, immediately below the smallest usable-contact count among the ten libraries (141,993,330). We rebuilt all ten `.hic` files and reran HiCCUPS at 5, 10, and 25 kb using the same settings, followed by the same `<2 Mb` restriction.

Depth normalization markedly reduced variation in loop yield. Across libraries, the coefficient of variation in total call count decreased from 0.335 to 0.163, a 51.5% reduction, and the range decreased from 6,017 to 1,360 calls, a 77.4% reduction. The Spearman correlation between original usable-contact depth and total loop count changed from 0.794 before downsampling to -0.115 after downsampling. Thus, sequencing depth was a major technical driver of the original between-library variation.

The analysis also revealed substantial depth sensitivity of individual calls. The full-depth pooled union contained 31,021 exact, resolution-specific call records, whereas the downsampled pooled union contained 14,414. Exactly 10,936 full-depth calls were recovered after downsampling (35.25%; pooled Jaccard index 0.317). Recovery was resolution dependent: 11.91% at 5 kb, 32.29% at 10 kb, and 50.25% at 25 kb. Allowing the standard HiCCUPS radius tolerance increased median sample-level recovery from 22.01% to 26.25%, without changing the conclusion that individual call identities were depth sensitive. Calls supported only by libraries above the median original depth had 13.65% exact recovery, compared with 57.96% for the remaining calls, indicating material influence of the higher-depth libraries on the full-depth union.

We agree that downsampling does not make pairwise strain comparisons biologically interpretable. Each strain is represented by one Hi-C library, so depth normalization cannot create biological replication or separate strain effects from other library-specific effects. We therefore remove the claims of conserved topology, biological divergence, and strain-specific chromatin architecture. We also do not perform or interpret a loop-versus-genetic-distance analysis, because such an analysis would remain underpowered and confounded under this design. The full-depth result is instead treated as a pooled union resource with source-library provenance; the 140M-contact union and recovery analysis are reported prominently as depth-sensitivity analyses. Calls observed in one source library are retained as resource records but are not called strain-specific biological loops.

### Major Comment 2

> The author’s decision to focus on loops that include at least 6 CTCF sites does not align with their stated goal of identifying loops that are likely to mediate enhancer-promoter interactions. It is well established that there are multiple types of loops, and that CTCF-associated looping are not the most relevant type enhancer promoter interaction. See for example, PMID: 39484446, from 4D nucleome project. Notably, that manuscript reports that loops most strongly characterized by insulator-insulator interactions (enriched for CTCF-anchors) are also the least likely to serve a gene regulatory function. Thus, the decision by the authors to focus on CTCF effectively excludes the loops that are most interesting regarding enhancer-promoter interactions.

**Response:** We agree and thank the reviewer for directing us to the 4D Nucleome study. The `CTCF >=6` criterion has been removed entirely from loop retention, promoter-enhancer assignment, evidence categories, confidence labels, highlighted subsets, gene ranking, and GO input construction. No regulatory category now depends on CTCF motif count.

The FIMO output is retained only as a descriptive structural sequence annotation. We will describe the historical output precisely as **3,191,859 exact-coordinate-deduplicated predicted CTCF motif intervals generated from 100 PWM models**, rather than as unique CTCF sites or experimentally observed CTCF binding sites. Overlapping predictions from multiple PWM models are not interpreted as independent biological loci, and the positional plots will be described as motif-density patterns near loop anchors rather than evidence of enhancer function. The recommended 4D Nucleome study will be cited when distinguishing structural/insulator-associated loops from putative regulatory contacts.

Regulatory evidence is now assigned independently using direct promoter/TSS overlap and TSS-excluded ATAC accessibility. This change preserves CTCF-associated calls in the general chromatin-loop resource while preventing CTCF from determining which calls are considered putative regulatory contacts.

### Major Comment 3

> Integration of this data with transcriptomic data or epigenomic data would allow for more meaningful conclusions about these loops.

**Response:** We agree. Sample-matched transcriptomic and active-enhancer histone-mark data are not available for these ten Hi-C libraries. We therefore integrated a published rat prefrontal-cortex snATAC-seq peak catalog as orthogonal epigenomic evidence and substantially restricted our functional claims. The external ATAC dataset is not matched to the Hi-C libraries by strain, sex, age, cell composition, or experimental preparation; accordingly, it is used only to annotate open chromatin and not to validate enhancer activity or regulatory function.

For the 12,295 calls with direct promoter/TSS annotation at exactly one anchor, 10,469 showed at least 50 bp of TSS-excluded ATAC overlap at the opposite anchor. To test whether this overlap exceeded what would be expected simply from anchor width and genomic context, we performed 1,000 matched-anchor permutations. Controls were matched by chromosome, resolution/anchor width, anchor side, and loop-distance bin. The observed versus matched-null overlap was 77.04% versus 73.28% at 5 kb, 83.57% versus 81.35% at 10 kb, and 89.49% versus 88.66% at 25 kb. Across resolutions, the observed rate was 85.15%, compared with 83.30% under the matched null (enrichment ratio 1.022; empirical `P = 0.001`). We therefore conclude that ATAC provides statistically detectable but modest, resolution-dependent open-chromatin support beyond matched Hi-C-anchor expectations.

#### Major Comment 3c

## Referee 2

### General Comment

> This manuscript presents a genome-wide catalogue of chromatin loops derived from Hi-C data in the frontal cortex of ten rat strains within the Hybrid Rat Diversity Panel (HRDP), with the goal of inferring promoter–enhancer (P–E) interactions. The authors integrate loop calls with predicted CTCF binding sites and gene annotations to derive a filtered set of ~15,000 “high-confidence” regulatory interactions.
>
> The study addresses a clear and important gap in rat functional genomics, and the generation of a multi-strain 3D genome dataset has the potential to represent a valuable community resource. The manuscript is well written and methodologically detailed.

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for recognizing the potential value of a rat 3D-genome resource. In response to both reviewers, we no longer describe approximately 15,000 high-confidence regulatory interactions. The resource is instead presented as 31,021 pooled, exact, resolution-specific HiCCUPS call records under 2 Mb with layered promoter/TSS, ATAC, predicted CTCF motif, resolution, loop-distance, source-library, and HiCCUPS QC annotations. Putative regulatory evidence is reported in categories and is not treated as functional validation.

### Comment 1

> The inclusion of ten genetically diverse strains is a major strength of the study, could the authors define what they mean by genetically diverse in this context?

**Response:** We agree that this term requires a precise definition. The ten libraries represent multiple inbred lineages within the HRDP, including classical inbred and HXB recombinant-inbred strains. We therefore evaluated their genomic relationships using autosomal SNP genotypes. We retained 22,854 biallelic variable SNPs with complete genotypes across all ten libraries, LD-pruned these to 1,509 markers, and calculated pairwise identity-by-state distances and principal components using PLINK 2.0. Pairwise IBS distances ranged from 0.121 to 0.535, and the structured separation in the PCA confirmed that the included strains have distinct genomic backgrounds. In the resubmission, we will define diversity using these SNP relationships together with HRDP breeding origin and documented genomic variation, cite the relevant HRDP references, and list the exact ten strains and their panel membership in the sample table. We do not infer that these ten libraries uniformly span the full genetic diversity of the entire HRDP, and this analysis is not used to test genotype-loop associations.

### Comment 2

> The analysis produces a consensus loop map, and strain-specific variation is largely attributed to technical variability (e.g. sequencing depth). Is it valuable to consider strain specific loops?

**Response:** The completed reanalysis shows that the pooled calls should not be called a consensus map and that single-library calls should not be interpreted as strain-specific biological loops. Downsampling reduced the loop-count coefficient of variation from 0.335 to 0.163, while exact call recovery remained strongly depth sensitive. Moreover, each strain has only one Hi-C library. We therefore define the primary result as a pooled union with source-library provenance, not as a consensus map. Single-library calls remain available as annotated records but are not used to claim strain-specific regulation, conservation, or divergence. Robust strain-specific inference would require biological replication and an analysis design capable of separating strain from library effects.

### Comment 3

> Do loop differences correlate with genetic distances between strains?

**Response:** We did not test or interpret this association in the final reanalysis. With one library per strain, a loop-versus-genetic-distance correlation would not separate genetic effects from technical or other library-specific effects. The downsampling analysis confirms substantial depth sensitivity, especially at 5 kb. We therefore consider a genetic-distance association under the current design under-supported and exclude it from the manuscript. A defensible analysis would require replicated libraries and validated strain-level genotype data in addition to depth normalization.

### Comment 4

> As strong correlation between sequencing depth and loop counts was identified did the authors consider whether it was necessary to normalise datasets to comparable sequencing depths

**Response:** Yes. We downsampled all ten duplicate-removed, MAPQ-filtered contact sets to exactly 140 million valid contacts, rebuilt the `.hic` files, and reran HiCCUPS at 5, 10, and 25 kb. The loop-count coefficient of variation decreased from 0.335 to 0.163, and the correlation between original usable-contact depth and loop count changed from 0.794 to -0.115. However, only 35.25% of the full-depth pooled exact calls were recovered after downsampling, and recovery was strongly resolution dependent. These results show that normalization substantially reduces technical variation in total call yield but does not make individual loop identities or downstream gene rankings fully stable. The downsampled union and recovery analysis will therefore be reported as a prominent sensitivity analysis, and no strain-specific inference will be made.

## Concluding Statement

The completed reanalysis directly addresses the principal analytical concerns regarding sequencing depth, CTCF-based filtering, promoter/TSS definition, ATAC background expectation, dual-promoter contacts, and cross-resolution sensitivity. It also identifies limitations that cannot be removed computationally: one library per strain, lack of sample-matched expression and active-enhancer data, lack of CTCF ChIP-seq, and lack of perturbational validation. The resubmission will therefore make narrower claims and present the work as a pooled chromatin-loop annotation resource with transparent evidence layers and sensitivity analyses.
